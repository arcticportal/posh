About POSH

The Polar Observing Site Hub (POSH) is a discovery portal that compiles details from multiple observing inventories for over 50,000 research and monitoring sites, such as: stations, facilities, plots, moorings, observatories, platforms, community-based observations, and wherever repeat measurements have been made. For each site, POSH displays location, site name, description, organization, contact information, associated networks, observed properties, and more. Overall, it is designed to provide a more comprehensive perspective on where and how observing takes place across the high latitudes.

POSH is not the original source for this information. Instead, it links to authoritative source catalogs:

Because POSH is an aggregator, it does not replace these catalogs. Instead, it relies on them and highlights the need for their continued funding and support. This approach is similar to how Expedia and other travel portals simultaneously combine results from multiple providers, and makes it easier for end users to find the information they need without searching across numerous disconnected resources.

POSH is currently a prototype. In other words, it is a test case and proof of concept for aggregating site metadata from an ecosystem of distributed nodes. Additional funding is being sought to add tens of thousands of sites from other inventories. The end goal is to have a sustainable discovery portal that is distributed, interoperable, extensive, and integrated.

POSH was created as a collaboration among the Sustaining Arctic Observing Networks initiative (SAON), the Arctic Portal, and the University of Colorado Boulder, with initial funding from SAON and the NOAA GOMO Arctic Research Program. It aligns with the vision of the SAON Polar Observing Assets Working Group (POAwg), which has a goal of making observing-related metadata more Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable (the FAIR Principles).
