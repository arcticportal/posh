Frequently Asked Questions

Why use POSH?

Because this tool searches across multiple inventories and dozens of networks simultaneously, it helps to meet the needs of a variety of users:

  • science planners to assess status, overlap, and gaps
  • network and logistics managers to co-locate or optimize limited resources
  • funding officers to avoid duplicated effort
  • researchers to collaborate with facilities and projects, to find data, or to calibrate and validate satellite imagery
  • and community members to discover, engage with, and leverage observational efforts and data collected nearby.

You can click on the provided links to the source catalogs for more complete information about each site.

What are the colors and circles on the map?

Each numbered circle on the map represents a cluster of many observing sites. The number of sites for each cluster (from tens to thousands) is shown by size (from small to large) and color (from blue to red). Clicking on a cluster or otherwise zooming in will reveal individual small purple circles, one for each site; clicking on these will open a site landing page in another tab.

How does POSH compile site information from the source catalogs?

Our software frequently harvests site information from open data streams hosted by the source catalogs. Site-level metadata records (typically in JSON) are ingested from their publicly accessible web services and Application Program Interfaces (APIs). The metadata records are locally cached in a relational database, and then harmonized by mapping across differing schemas to a common subset of fields before presentation with our frontend web framework.

Why are there duplicate entries for some sites?

A fraction of the observing sites in POSH are documented in more than one of the source catalogs. These sites tend to be high-profile infrastructures such as the Abisko Scientific Research Station or Toolik Field Station. However, the duplicate entries are not identical; instead, the various source catalogs provide complementary information. For this reason, the duplicate entries are preserved in POSH.

How can we add our site inventory to POSH?

Please reach out via the Contact page. We hope to add more source catalogs—as long as they have machine-readable endpoints or CSV files etc. that can be harvested automatically.

What is the difference between POSH and RoPON?

POSH focuses on research and monitoring sites, whereas a companion tool—the Registry of Polar Observing Networks (RoPON)—provides information about observing networks. They may look similar but serve different purposes.
